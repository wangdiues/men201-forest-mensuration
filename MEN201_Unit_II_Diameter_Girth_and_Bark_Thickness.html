Geometry · Conversion

For a circle, G = πD

DIAMETER + GIRTH
G = 2πr
D = 2r
G = πD   ·   D = G/π
Quick check

A tree has G = 30 cm

Substitute
D = 30/π
Calculate
D ≈ 9.55 cm
Reverse check

If D = 1 cm, then G = π × 1 ≈ 3.14 cm.

Geometry · From diameter to area

Cross-sectional area grows with the square of the diameter

DIAMETER + GIRTH
BA = πD²/4 = πr²
BA (m²) = 0.00007854 × D²when D is entered in centimetres
Why it matters

Basal area at breast height is the standard expression of stand density, and it is the bridge from a single diameter reading to volume.

Calipers · Error profile

Fast direct readings—until size, dirt or terrain works against you

Advantages

  • Quick with little calculation
  • Positive and negative placement errors may partly offset
  • Can be used by trained field assistants
Limitations
  • Misadjustment produces inaccurate readings
  • Large calipers are awkward to carry
  • Two orientations are difficult on steep ground
  • Wet or dirty beams can jam the movable arm

14 / 42
Measuring tape · Error profile

Portable and consistent, but rough bark tends to inflate the reading

DIAMETER + GIRTH

Why crews like it

  • Compact and easy to carry
  • One encircling measurement
  • Works around logs on the ground
  • Systematic positive bias can be calibrated
Error signature

Tape follows the convex hull of bark irregularities. Deep fissures are bridged, so the circular-equivalent diameter is usually overestimated.

Where it struggles

  • Rough bark exaggerates girth
  • Slower in dense stands
  • The far side cannot be seen
  • Hard to keep level on leaning or obstructed stems
  • Different operators may apply different tension
Source: Session 3, slide 10.
15 / 42
Diameter tape · The printed conversion

The tape measures girth but the scale reports G ÷ π

DIAMETER + GIRTH
D/G = 1/π ≈ 0.3183
3.14159 cm of tape = 1.00 cm diameter
Design logic

Each diameter unit is spaced π units apart on the physical tape, so no field calculation is needed.

Benefits
  • Direct diameter reading
  • Shorter scale than an ordinary tape
  • Convenient for repeated inventory work
Caution

The circular conversion assumes a perfect circle; irregular tree cross-sections can require species- or stand-specific calibration.

Source: Session 3, slides 11–12.

Diameter tape · Biological calibration

Tree cross-sections are not perfect circles, so D/G can vary

DIAMETER + GIRTH
SpeciesD/G factor
Pinus roxburghii0.3093
Tectona grandis0.3160
Shorea robusta0.3119
Perfect circle0.3183
Example32 cm × 0.3183 ≈ 10.19 cm

A lower factor means the measured perimeter is larger than a circle with the same representative diameter.

Field implication

Use a calibration factor only when the inventory manual specifies the species, region and derivation. Otherwise report the standard circular-equivalent diameter.

Indirect measurement · Angle instead of contact

The relascope reads the angle a stem subtends

DIAMETER + GIRTH
Principle

Sighted from a fixed point, a stem covers an angle set by its diameter and its distance. With the distance known, the instrument's scale converts that angle straight into a diameter.

Where it earns its place
  • Upper-stem diameters that cannot be reached
  • Angle-count sampling for basal area per hectare
  • Rapid stand-level work without touching every tree
The trade

No contact error — but it needs a clear line of sight, an accurate distance and a practised observer.

DBH · The 1.3 m convention

A convenient height becomes powerful because everyone uses it

BREAST HEIGHT
  • Convenient working height with less fatigue
  • Often above undergrowth and basal swelling
  • Usually above buttress or abnormal basal growth
  • Independent of irregular stump height after felling
  • Internationally recognised reference point
Core principle

Comparability matters more than personal convenience. Mark and record any justified departure from 1.3 m.

Source: Session 3, slide 14.

DBH · Buttresses and surface preparation

Measure above persistent buttress and document the new height

BREAST HEIGHT

Buttressed species

Choose a point where the buttress no longer influences the stem and is not expected to extend during the monitoring interval.

Permanent plots

Record the cross-mark height so future crews return to exactly the same point.

Before measuring

  • Remove moss, creepers and lichens
  • Remove loose bark without cutting sound bark
  • Do not include vines or other objects under the tape
  • Mark the reference point with a cross
  • Record diameter in cm (or inches) and girth in m (or feet), following the field form

Bark · Definition and variation

Thickness is the distance from the outer surface to the cambium boundary

BARK THICKNESS
one-sided t

Bark is not a fixed correction. It varies at four scales.

  • Among species
  • Within a species
  • Among trees with age and locality
  • Along one tree, commonly thicker near the base than near the tip

Bark · Why the correction matters

Over-bark diameter includes material that may not belong in wood volume

BARK THICKNESS
Volume responds to D²a small diameter bias becomes a larger area and volume bias
Boundary question

Are you estimating total stem volume over bark, or merchantable wood volume under bark?

Two trees can share the same DOB

The tree with thicker bark has a smaller wood cylinder. Without a bark correction, their under-bark volumes would be treated as equal.

Thereforedefine the boundary before computing

Bark · Sampling non-uniform thickness

Measure two opposite pairs that match the diameter axes

BARK THICKNESS
1
Align with the first diameter

Measure bark at its two opposite endpoints.

2
Rotate 90°

Measure the second opposite pair.

3
Average explicitly

Mean one-sided thickness: t̄ = (t₁+t₂+t₃+t₄)/4.

Equivalent double bark

2t̄ is the mean bark contribution across a diameter.

Bark · Diameter conversion

Subtract bark from both sides of the diameter

Dub = Dob − 2t̄
Dub = Dob − bwhere b = 2t̄ is double bark thickness
Notation check

Do not subtract a one-sided thickness only once. A diameter crosses bark at two endpoints.

32 / 42
Bark · Girth conversion

Reducing radius by t reduces circumference by 2πt

BARK THICKNESS
Over bark
Gob = 2πrob
Under-bark radius
rub = rob − t
Substitute
Gub = 2π(rob − t)
Expand
Gub = 2πrob − 2πt
Gub = Gob − 2πt

Here t is the mean one-sided radial bark thickness.

Unit discipline

G and t must be in the same length unit before subtraction.

Source: Session 4, slide 7.
33 / 42
Bark · Expressing the correction as a share

7.5% of the diameter is 14.4% of the area

BARK THICKNESS
bark % of diameter = (2t̄ / Dob) × 100
bark % of area = [1 − (Dub/Dob)²] × 100
Read it as

Bark is a ring, not a slab. Stripping a thin outer ring removes a disproportionate share of the cross-section, because area follows D².

Field use

Bark percentage is how species-level bark corrections are tabulated, so a crew can estimate under-bark volume without gauging every tree.

The same 40 cm tree
Diameter o.b.
Dob = 40.0 cm
Double bark
2t̄ = 3.0 cm
Diameter u.b.
Dub = 37.0 cm
Share of diameter
3.0 / 40.0 = 7.5%
Share of area
1 − (37/40)² = 14.4%
Lessonthin bark, thick correction

Quality control · Errors with consequences

Most mistakes are not arithmetic—they are placement and definition

FIELD WORKFLOW
Placement errors
  • Measuring from the downhill side
  • Tape tilted rather than perpendicular
  • Caliper axis not representative
  • Measuring directly on swelling or fork
  • Including vines or loose bark
Definition + recording errors
  • Confusing G with D
  • Omitting units
  • Not stating o.b. or u.b.
  • Subtracting t only once from DOB
  • Failing to record shifted measurement height
