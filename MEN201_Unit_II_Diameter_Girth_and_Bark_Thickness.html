Why the exponent matters more than the unit

Length, area and volume are L, L² and L³. Halve a diameter reading by mistake and the area is quartered and the volume divided by eight. Dimensional errors do not stay small.

Tools · Contact geometry

Straight span, sliding jaws, or a loop around the stem

DIAMETER + GIRTH
Scale

Useful on stumps, log ends, and successive sections in stem analysis.

Calliper

A fixed arm, movable arm and graduated scale read diameter directly.

Tape

Wraps the whole circumference; keep it flat, untwisted and perpendicular.

Three tools, three error signatures

A scale needs a visible cross-section. A calliper needs two axes and square jaws. A tape needs a flat, level, untwisted loop. Choose the tool whose failure mode you can see and control.

Callipers · Error profile

Fast direct readings—until size, dirt or terrain works against you

DIAMETER + GIRTH
Advantages
  • Direct diameter reading in cm or mm
  • Quick with little calculation
  • Positive and negative placement errors may partly offset
  • Can be used by trained field assistants
Limitations
  • Misadjustment produces inaccurate readings
  • Large callipers are awkward to carry
  • Two orientations are difficult on steep ground

The advantages are about speed; the limitations are about bias. Speed can be recovered by practice. Bias cannot be recovered at all once the crew has left the plot.

Measuring tape · Technique

A tape is accurate only when its plane is perpendicular to the stem

DIAMETER + GIRTH
flat · untwisted · level
1
Inspect the tape

Do not use a stretched, broken or illegible tape.

2
Clear the path

Exclude vines and undergrowth; remove only loose surface material.

3
Control tension

Use consistent tension and ensure the zero and reading marks meet correctly.

The one that catches everybody

A tape that is not perpendicular to the stem measures an ellipse, and an ellipse is always longer than the circle. The error is one-directional, so it never averages out.

Tape technique after West (2009), ch. 4. The tension and levelling rules are common to all published inventory manuals.
14 / 48
Measuring tape · Error profile

Portable and consistent, but rough bark tends to inflate the reading

DIAMETER + GIRTH

Why crews like it

  • Compact and easy to carry
  • One encircling measurement
  • Works around logs on the ground
  • Systematic positive bias can be calibrated
Error signature

Tape follows the convex hull of bark irregularities. Deep fissures are bridged, so the circular-equivalent diameter is usually overestimated.

Where it struggles

  • Rough bark exaggerates girth
  • Slower in dense stands
  • The far side cannot be seen
  • Hard to keep level on leaning or obstructed stems
  • Different operators may apply different tension
Tape bias after Husch, Beers & Kershaw (2003), ch. 4. The convex-hull argument follows directly from the geometry.

Diameter tape · The printed conversion

The tape measures girth but the scale reports G ÷ π

DIAMETER + GIRTH
D/G = 1/π ≈ 0.3183
3.14159 cm of tape = 1.00 cm diameter
Design logic

Each diameter unit is spaced π units apart on the physical tape, so no field calculation is needed.

Benefits
  • Direct diameter reading
  • Shorter scale than an ordinary tape
  • Convenient for repeated inventory work
Caution

The circular conversion assumes a perfect circle; irregular tree cross-sections can require species- or stand-specific calibration.

Diameter-tape design. The printed scale divides girth by π; see West (2009), ch. 4.

Indirect measurement · Angle instead of contact

The relascope reads the angle a stem subtends

DIAMETER + GIRTH
Principle

Sighted from a fixed point, a stem covers an angle set by its diameter and its distance. With the distance known, the instrument's scale converts that angle straight into a diameter.

Where it earns its place
  • Upper-stem diameters that cannot be reached
  • Angle-count sampling for basal area per hectare
  • Rapid stand-level work without touching every tree
The trade

No contact error — but it needs a clear line of sight, an accurate distance and a practised observer.

Background only — the relascope and wedge prism are not part of the revised module and are not assessed. Angle-count sampling is developed in Unit VI. Bitterlich (1984), The Relascope Idea.

DBH · Breast height is protocol-dependent

A convenient height becomes powerful only because everyone uses the same one

BREAST HEIGHT
  • Convenient working height with less fatigue
  • Often above undergrowth and basal swelling
  • Usually above buttress or abnormal basal growth
  • Independent of irregular stump height after felling
The value itself is set by the protocol

Bhutan’s Second National Forest Inventory recorded over-bark DBH at 1.37 m. Many international manuals use 1.30 m. Neither is universally correct.

Core principle

Identify and follow the applicable inventory protocol, and record the reference height you used. Comparability matters more than personal convenience — mark and record any justified departure.

DBH reference height is protocol-dependent. Bhutan, Second National Forest Inventory (DoFPS 2023): over-bark DBH at 1.37 m. Many international manuals: 1.30 m.

Bark · Tools

A gauge cuts to the boundary; a probe locates it

BARK THICKNESS
Bark gauge

A calibrated cutting edge is driven through bark until resistance changes at the wood/cambium boundary.

Bark probe

A slender probe penetrates the layer and indicates the depth to the wood boundary.

Both tools measure to the same boundary

The gauge cuts to the cambium; the probe finds it. Either is acceptable — but a crew that mixes the two without saying so has introduced a difference nobody can later quantify.

Bark gauge and probe. See West (2009), Tree and Forest Measurement, 2nd ed., ch. 4.

Bark · Expressing the correction as a share

7.5% of the diameter is 14.4% of the area

BARK THICKNESS
bark % of diameter = (2t̄ / Dob) × 100
bark % of area = [1 − (Dub/Dob)²] × 100
Read it as

Bark is a ring, not a slab. Stripping a thin outer ring removes a disproportionate share of the cross-section, because area follows D².

Field use

Bark percentage is how species-level bark corrections are tabulated, so a crew can estimate under-bark volume without gauging every tree.

A 9.8 cm stem is not a small tree

Quality control · calibration and repeatability

A stretched tape gives a more precise wrong answer

QUALITY CONTROL
Calibrate — start of every season, and after any drop
  • Girth and diameter tapes stretch with use. Check against a steel rule over a known 1 m span; retire a tape that reads more than 2 mm out
  • Steel tapes do not stretch appreciably but kink, and the zero end wears
  • Callipers go out of square. Check the movable arm reads zero when closed and stays parallel across the beam
  • Bark gauges — check the scale zero against the cutting edge
Why calibration and not more measurements

An instrument error is systematic: the same sign on every tree, so it survives averaging intact. Only calibration removes it.

Working tolerances for this module
DBH, blind re-measurewithin 0.3 cm
Girth, blind re-measurewithin 1.0 cm
Bark thickness, one sidewithin 2 mm
Reference-height markwithin 2 cm

Working values for a teaching exercise. For real work the binding tolerances come from the field manual governing that inventory.

1 · Blind

Student A measures, records, and covers the sheet. Student B measures independently. Only then compare. If B can see A’s number the exercise measures nothing but social pressure.

2 · Compare

Record both values and the difference. Re-measure 5–10 % of trees this way — it is what allows the error to be quantified rather than guessed.

3 · Outside tolerance

Do not average and move on. Find the cause: usually the mark has moved, the tape is twisted, or the two are measuring different axes on an oval stem.

Quality-control practice common to published inventory manuals; the systematic/random distinction is Unit I, and the same discipline reappears in Unit VI.

Official Bhutan policy context · why this unit exists

Diameter in Bhutanese forest management

DIAMETER + GIRTH

Diameter is the most-measured quantity in Bhutanese forestry, and the first measurement in every chain that follows.

Community-forest inventory

A group measures its own stems, usually with a girth tape, to prepare and revise its management plan — and the numbers decide how much timber it may take each year.

Timber marking

Trees marked for felling are selected and scaled on diameter. The rural timber allotment that follows is a volume derived from it.

NFI tree measurement

Every tree of 10 cm DBH and above in every plot of the national grid — the raw material of the State of Forest Report.

Biomass and carbon

The Bhutan allometric equations of Unit VI take basal area as their predictor. Diameter is where a national carbon figure begins.

Why this unit carries more weight than it looks

Diameter enters basal area as a square, and volume as roughly a square. A 1 % error in diameter is about 2 % in basal area — and in every volume, biomass and carbon figure downstream. This is the most consequential unit in the module because it is the earliest.

Beyond timber

The same tape readings describe diameter-class diversity, large habitat trees, deadwood, regeneration and change between inventories. Diameter is not a timber measurement; it is a forest-structure measurement that timber happens to use.

National Forest Policy of Bhutan 2011 · Forest and Nature Conservation Act of Bhutan 2023 · DoFPS, State of Forest Report 2023

Quality control · Errors with consequences

Most mistakes are not arithmetic—they are placement and definition

FIELD WORKFLOW
Placement errors
  • Measuring from the downhill side
  • Tape tilted rather than perpendicular
  • Calliper axis not representative
  • Measuring directly on swelling or fork
  • Including vines or loose bark
Definition + recording errors
  • Confusing G with D
  • Omitting units
  • Not stating o.b. or u.b.
  • Subtracting t only once from DOB
  • Failing to record shifted measurement height
Why this list is worth more than the formulas

Not one of these is an arithmetic mistake. They are errors of placement and definition, and they are invisible in the data — which is exactly why a field sheet records the height, the axis, the boundary and the exception code.

Error classification follows Unit I: random error is reduced by repetition, systematic error is not.

That one rule resolves most of the confusion in the literature: g is the tree in front of you, G is the hectare it stands in. Likewise v and V.

Two conversions that follow
C = πd  ·  d = C / π
Dub = Dob − 2t
Gub = Gob − 2πt

A diameter crosses bark twice. Subtract 2t, never t.

Crown terms — four names

Crown-base height ground to the base of the live crown · crown length H − crown-base height · crown midpoint height ground to the crown midpoint · live-crown ratio crown length ÷ H. The bare term “crown height” is not used — three incompatible meanings.

Not a symbol, but it belongs here

DBH reference height is protocol-dependent. Bhutan’s Second NFI used 1.37 m; many international manuals use 1.30 m. Follow the applicable protocol and record the height used.
