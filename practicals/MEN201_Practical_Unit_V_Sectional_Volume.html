Sectional Volume of a Felled Tree — field sheet

Unit V
Practical
Aim. Obtain the volume of one felled blue pine by sectional measurement, and compare it with the volume the Department's Model 16 merchantable-volume equation predicts from the same tree's DBH and height. Part of the difference is definition, not error — Model 16 predicts merchantable volume to a 10 cm top, with branches excluded.

Safety before anything else

Felling is carried out only by the authorised crew. Students remain at a minimum of two tree lengths until the tree is on the ground and the crew signals that it is safe to approach.

Sectional measurement Huber's formula — one mid-diameter per section

SecFrom mTo mLength L m Mid-diameter dm cmBark t̄ cm Sectional area s = πdm²/4 m²Volume v = s·L m³

From and To exist so that a suspicious reading can be found again and re-checked. Without them it cannot. The terminal section above the last full length is scaled as a cone: v = s·L/3.

g(X) = (X−t₁)³₊ − (X−t₂)³₊·(t₃−t₁)/(t₃−t₂) + (X−t₃)³₊·(t₂−t₁)/(t₃−t₂). A bracket that comes out negative contributes zero. Fitted range: DBH about 10–128 cm, height 4.6–61.3 m.

Is this tree inside the calibration range?   Y / N    If N, say so beside every figure you report from it.

In the report, separate the three causes of disagreement. Definition — Model 16 stops at a 10 cm top and excludes branches, so it should read lower than a full sectional measurement of the whole stem. Model error — the equation's own error, which does not shrink by measuring more trees. Measurement error — in d and h, and d enters squared.
