General objective

To make students competent in measuring tree diameter, girth, crown characteristics, height, bark thickness, volume, age, growth, and stand-level parameters — for felled and for standing trees.

Pre-requisite

None. The module assumes no prior measurement training, and begins with units, geometry and the meaning of accuracy before any instrument is handled.

Original descriptor March 2022. Revised 07/08/2026 to bring in current forest-inventory methods, digital data management, biomass and carbon estimation, remote-measurement technologies, and spherical-densiometer crown-closure assessment. Revised by Wangdi.

General objective

MEN 201 · Forest Mensuration
Module descriptor

To make students competent in measuring tree diameter, girth, crown characteristics, height, bark thickness, volume, age, growth and stand-level parameters, for felled and for standing trees.

Instruments the module covers
  • Diameter tapes, linear measuring tapes and callipers
  • Bark gauges
  • Clinometers, hypsometers and laser rangefinders
  • Digital hypsometers
  • Spherical densiometers
  • Compasses and GPS/GNSS devices
  • Digital forest-data collection tools
Introduced as supplementary areas
  • Forest inventory and sampling
  • Digital data management
  • Biomass and carbon estimation
  • Remote sensing
  • Drones and LiDAR
  • Emerging forest-measurement technologies

Both conventional and modern instruments are taught. The supplementary areas enrich the approved core; they do not replace the fundamental principles or the practical skills of forest mensuration.

What the weighting is telling you

Sixty per cent of this module is earned outdoors, with an instrument in your hands, before the written paper is sat. Each practical is marked in three equal parts of ten marks, so no part of either can be neglected and carried by the rest.

Supplementary topics may be used for discussion, demonstration, exercises, examples or integrated practical activities. They are included in formal examinations or graded assessments only when approved by the relevant academic authority.

Module descriptor · list of practicals · the eight core exercises

Practicals 1 to 8 · core

MEN 201 · Forest Mensuration
Module descriptor
1
Diameter and girth. Diameter tape, calliper and linear tape; breast-height rules; different stem and terrain conditions; girth to diameter; compare instruments; sources of error; approved field form.
5
Canopy cover — the national procedure. Establish the 12.62 m plot from a supplied centre; lay the pre-generated 2.5 m grid; record 89 + 11 points with the GRS crown densitometer; calculate cover for one plot and pooled; classify as forest / high forest. Comparative: hold and level the spherical densiometer, record in the required directions, and make an independent visual estimate; compare all three, explain the differences; short technical report.
2
Bark thickness. Identify measurement points; use a bark gauge; double bark thickness; diameter and girth over and under bark; sources of error.
6
Log volume. Length, large-end, small-end and mid-diameters; Huber's, Smalian's, Newton's and quarter-girth formulae; compare the volumes and explain the differences.
3
Tree height. Clinometer, tape, hypsometer, and laser rangefinder or digital hypsometer where available; observation positions; horizontal distance; repeat and compare; discuss error.
7
Standing-tree volume. Diameter and height; select a form factor; individual-tree and merchantable volume; over and under bark; use a volume table and compare.
4
Crown characteristics. Crown width on two axes; mean crown diameter and projection area; height to the live crown base; live-crown length and ratio; classify by crown position.
8
Tree age and increment. Age from stump sections or visible rings; increment borer where available; handle and label cores; diameter increment, CAI, PAI and MAI; limitations and error.

Practicals 9 to 13 · supplementary

MEN 201 · Forest Mensuration
Module descriptor
9
Basic forest inventory. Define the objective and select a design; plot size and shape; establish a fixed-area plot; GPS coordinates where available; number and identify trees; measure diameter, height, crown variables and defects; calculate trees per hectare, mean diameter, mean height, basal area per hectare and stand volume; interpret.

Module descriptor · how the supplementary modules are introduced

Supplementary teaching approach

MEN 201 · Forest Mensuration
Module descriptor

Supplementary modules are introduced after students have acquired the fundamental skills needed to measure individual trees.

The suggested sequence
  • Teach the prescribed theoretical concept
  • Demonstrate the relevant instrument or method
  • Conduct a field exercise
  • Record the measurements on paper or digital forms
  • Analyse the data using a spreadsheet
  • Interpret the results in relation to forest management
  • Introduce the related modern technology
Worked through as pairs
  • Diameter and height measurement → a sample-plot inventory
  • Volume estimation → biomass and carbon estimation
  • Crown-closure measurement → drone and remote-sensing assessment
  • Tree-growth measurement → permanent sample plots and repeated monitoring

Supplementary topics are taught through short lectures, demonstrations, guided exercises, case studies, sample datasets and integrated field activities. They enrich the approved core content without replacing the fundamental principles and practical skills of forest mensuration.

Field safety and data quality

MEN 201 · Forest Mensuration
Module descriptor
Field safety and professional practice — students must
  • Follow institutional field-safety procedures
  • Wear appropriate clothing and footwear
  • Use instruments according to their operating instructions
  • Avoid damage to trees during measurement
  • Handle increment borers and other sharp equipment safely
  • Work in assigned groups in difficult terrain
  • Maintain communication with the lecturer or field supervisor
  • Protect instruments from rain, impact and improper storage
  • Respect forest-management regulations and local community requirements
  • Record field observations honestly and accurately
  • Avoid fabrication or alteration of field data
  • Report damaged equipment and unsafe conditions immediately
Data quality requirements — students must
  • Use consistent measurement units
  • Record measurements immediately
  • Use clear plot and tree identifiers
  • Avoid overwriting original observations without documentation
  • Repeat doubtful measurements
  • Check instrument calibration
  • Review field sheets before leaving the plot
  • Identify missing observations and check unrealistic values
  • Maintain original and cleaned versions of datasets
  • Document corrections and back up digital data
  • State clearly the assumptions used in calculations
  • Report limitations and uncertainty

Module descriptor · the fifteen notes governing the supplementary modules

Notes on the supplementary modules

MEN 201 · Forest Mensuration
Module descriptor
Status and priority
  • Units VIII to XI are supplementary enrichment components
  • They connect traditional mensuration to current inventory and monitoring practice
  • Their inclusion must not reduce the time needed to develop competence in fundamental tree-measurement skills
  • Practical exposure is prioritised over highly detailed theoretical treatment of advanced technologies
  • Inventory exercises should emphasise fixed-area sample plots
Instruments
  • The relascope and wedge prism are not included in the revised module
  • Canopy cover assessment follows the Forest Canopy Cover Assessment Guidelines 2024 using the GRS crown densitometer; the spherical densiometer is retained as a comparative instrument
  • Laser rangefinders and digital hypsometers may supplement clinometer-based height measurement
  • Where equipment is unavailable, demonstrations, datasets, photographs, videos and case studies may be used
Assessment and change
  • Supplementary modules are formally examined or given marks only when approved through the appropriate academic process
  • Supplementary content may be adjusted for available time, equipment, student readiness, institutional priorities and field conditions
  • The adjunct lecturer coordinates with the programme leader or module coordinator before changing formally assessed content
  • Data-analysis exercises use software available to the institution and accessible to students
  • Drones and LiDAR are introduced conceptually unless equipment, software, trained personnel and practical time are all available
  • All field activities follow institutional safety requirements
