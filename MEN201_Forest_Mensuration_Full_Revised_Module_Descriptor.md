# General Objective

The aim of the module is to make students competent in measuring tree diameter, girth, crown characteristics, height, bark thickness, volume, age, growth, and stand-level parameters for felled and standing trees.

Students will learn to use conventional and modern forest-measurement instruments, including diameter tapes, linear measuring tapes, callipers, bark gauges, clinometers, hypsometers, laser rangefinders, digital hypsometers, spherical densiometers, compasses, GPS/GNSS devices, and digital forest-data collection tools.

The module also introduces students to forest inventory and sampling, digital data management, biomass and carbon estimation, remote sensing, drones, LiDAR, and emerging forest-measurement technologies as supplementary areas of learning.

# Learning Outcomes

On completion of the module, students will be able to:

1\. Explain the definition, scope, objectives, and applications of forest mensuration.

2\. Measure the height, girth, bark thickness, diameter, crown characteristics, and volume of trees and logs.

3\. Correctly handle and maintain instruments used for measuring diameter, girth, height, bark thickness, and crown closure.

4\. Identify, evaluate, and minimize sources of error in forest measurements.

5\. Classify trees into appropriate girth and diameter classes.

6\. Assess canopy cover using Bhutan's Forest Canopy Cover Assessment Guidelines 2024 and the GRS crown densitometer, compare the result with visual and spherical-densiometer estimates, and express all three as percentages.

7\. Calculate the volume of standing trees, logs, and sawn timber using appropriate methods and formulae.

8\. Use general and local volume tables to estimate the volume of individual trees and forest stands.

9\. Measure tree and timber defects and calculate cull percentage and net usable volume.

10\. Determine the age of standing and felled trees using appropriate methods.

11\. Calculate tree growth and increment, including current annual increment, periodic annual increment, and mean annual increment.

12\. Establish basic forest inventory sample plots using appropriate sampling methods.

13\. Calculate stand parameters, including trees per hectare, mean diameter, mean height, basal area, stocking, and stand volume.

14\. Use GPS/GNSS, laser-based instruments, mobile applications, and spreadsheet software for forest measurement and data management.

15\. Organize, clean, summarize, analyze, interpret, and present forest mensuration data.

16\. Estimate above-ground biomass and forest carbon stocks using appropriate equations and conversion factors.

17\. Explain the applications and limitations of remote sensing, drones, LiDAR, GIS, and other emerging technologies in forest mensuration.

18\. Prepare and present a basic technical forest inventory report.

# Learning and Teaching Approach

The module will be delivered through lectures, classroom exercises, instrument demonstrations, field practicals, group assignments, independent study, data-analysis exercises, technical report writing, student presentations, case studies, and supplementary demonstrations of modern forestry technologies.

The teaching approach will emphasize the connection between theoretical principles, field measurements, data analysis, and practical forest-management applications. Students will first learn the fundamental principles of measurement before applying them through practical exercises in forest stands.

# Assessment Approach

## A. Individual Practical on Tree Measurement — 30%

Each student will undertake an individual practical exercise within the CNR campus or another suitable forest area. The practical will focus on tree-height measurement, diameter measurement, instrument handling, data recording, and measurement accuracy.

**1. Tree-height estimation using a clinometer — 10 marks**

**Students will:**

- Select a suitable observation position.
- Measure horizontal distance using a measuring tape or laser rangefinder.
- Measure angles or percentage readings to the top and base of a tree.
- Apply appropriate geometric or trigonometric methods.
- Calculate total tree height.
- Record observations systematically.
- Identify possible sources of measurement error.

**2. Tree-height estimation using a hypsometer or laser-based instrument — 10 marks**

**Students will:**

- Correctly handle a hypsometer, laser rangefinder, or digital hypsometer.
- Select a suitable observation distance.
- Measure tree height using the instrument.
- Repeat measurements to assess consistency.
- Compare results with clinometer-based measurements.
- Explain differences between manual and digital measurement methods.

Where laser-based equipment is unavailable, this component may be conducted using another approved height-measurement method or a demonstration dataset.

**3. Diameter measurement and accuracy assessment — 10 marks**

**Students will:**

- Measure diameter at breast height using a diameter tape.
- Measure the same trees using a calliper.
- Apply standard breast-height measurement rules.
- Compare measurements obtained using different instruments.
- Calculate measurement differences.
- Identify sources of variation and error.
- Record the results using an approved field form.

## B. Group Practical Assignment on Crown Closure — 30%

Students will work in groups of approximately five members. Each group will assess crown closure within the CNR campus or another suitable forest area using a spherical densiometer and visual assessment methods.

**1. Crown-closure assessment design — 10 marks**

**Students will:**

- Define the study population.
- Identify the forest area to be assessed.
- Select an appropriate sampling method.
- Determine the number and location of observation points.
- Explain the procedure for using a spherical densiometer.
- Develop a suitable field-recording format.

**2. Crown-closure measurements, analysis, and report — 10 marks**

**Students will:**

- Conduct crown-closure observations using a spherical densiometer.
- Take observations in the required directions at each sampling point.
- Conduct visual estimates at the same observation points.
- Calculate crown-closure percentages.
- Calculate average crown closure for the study area.
- Compare densiometer and visual estimates.
- Interpret the results.
- Prepare a written technical report.

**3. Presentation and group performance — 10 marks**

**Assessment will consider:**

- Correctness of the sampling method.
- Correct handling of the spherical densiometer.
- Accuracy of calculations.
- Quality of data presentation.
- Interpretation of findings.
- Quality of the written report.
- Clarity of oral presentation.
- Group participation and coordination.

## C. Semester-End Examination — 40%

Students will take a written examination of two hours covering the approved subject matter of the module.

The examination will be marked out of 100 and converted to 40 percent when calculating the final module mark.

Supplementary topics may be used for classroom discussion, demonstrations, exercises, examples, or integrated practical activities. Supplementary topics should only be included in formal examinations or graded assessments when approved by the relevant academic authority.

## Practical 4: Crown-Characteristics Measurement

**Students will:**

- Measure crown width along two axes.
- Calculate mean crown diameter and crown-projection area.
- Measure height to the base of the live crown.
- Calculate live-crown length and live-crown ratio.
- Classify trees according to crown position or crown class.

## Practical 5: Canopy Cover Assessment — the National Procedure, with Comparative Methods

**Students will:**

- Establish a 500 m² circular plot of 12.62 m radius from a supplied plot centre.
- Lay the 2.5 m × 2.5 m observation grid from a pre-generated point layout, without selecting
  their own points.
- Record 89 systematic and 11 additional observations with the GRS crown densitometer, taking the
  11 from the quadrant assigned to the group.
- Apply the binary tally rule and calculate canopy cover for a single plot and for the pooled plots.
- Classify the plot as forest or non-forest and as high forest or not, and state what follows under
  the Forest and Nature Conservation Act 2023.
- **As comparative methods:** correctly hold and level the spherical densiometer, conduct and record
  observations in the required directions, calculate crown-closure percentage, and make an
  independent visual estimate.
- Compare the three figures, explain why they differ, and identify sources of error.
- Prepare a short technical report.

*The spherical densiometer is retained as a comparative instrument. The prescribed method for the
assessment itself is the Forest Canopy Cover Assessment Guidelines 2024.*

## Practical 6: Log-Volume Measurement

**Students will:**

- Measure log length and large-end, small-end, and mid-diameters.
- Apply Huber’s, Smalian’s, Newton’s, and quarter-girth formulae as appropriate.
- Compare the calculated volumes and explain differences among estimates.

1\. Define the inventory objective and describe the forest population.

2\. Select an appropriate sampling method and establish one or more fixed-area sample plots.

3\. Record plot locations using GPS/GNSS where available.

4\. Identify tree species and measure diameter, height, bark thickness where required, crown characteristics, crown closure, and defects.

5\. Calculate trees per hectare, mean diameter, mean height, basal area per hectare, stand volume, biomass, and carbon stock.

6\. Enter, clean, and analyze the data using spreadsheet software.

7\. Prepare tables, graphs, and a basic plot-location map where possible.

8\. Interpret the findings and identify limitations and sources of uncertainty.

9\. Prepare a short technical inventory report and present the methods, results, limitations, and recommendations.

# Supplementary Teaching Approach

The supplementary modules should be introduced after students have acquired the fundamental skills required to measure individual trees.

The suggested teaching sequence is:

1\. Teach the prescribed theoretical concept.

2\. Demonstrate the relevant instrument or method.

3\. Conduct a field exercise.

4\. Record the measurements using paper or digital forms.

5\. Analyze the data using a spreadsheet.

6\. Interpret the results in relation to forest management.

7\. Introduce the related modern technology.

For example:

- Diameter and height measurement may be followed by a sample-plot inventory.
- Volume estimation may be followed by biomass and carbon estimation.
- Crown-closure measurement may be followed by an introduction to drone and remote-sensing assessment.
- Tree-growth measurement may be followed by an introduction to permanent sample plots and repeated monitoring.

# Field Safety and Professional Practice

Students must:

- Follow institutional field-safety procedures.
- Wear appropriate clothing and footwear.
- Use instruments according to operating instructions.
- Avoid damage to trees during measurement.
- Handle increment borers and other sharp equipment safely.
- Work in assigned groups in difficult terrain.
- Maintain communication with the lecturer or field supervisor.
- Protect instruments from rain, impact, and improper storage.
- Respect forest-management regulations and local community requirements.
- Record field observations honestly and accurately.
- Avoid fabrication or alteration of field data.
- Report damaged equipment and unsafe conditions immediately.

# Data Quality Requirements

Students must:

- Use consistent measurement units.
- Record measurements immediately.
- Use clear plot and tree identifiers.
- Avoid overwriting original observations without documentation.
- Repeat doubtful measurements.
- Check instrument calibration.
- Review field sheets before leaving the plot.
- Identify missing observations and check unrealistic values.
- Maintain original and cleaned versions of datasets.
- Document corrections and back up digital data.
- Clearly state assumptions used in calculations.
- Report limitations and uncertainty.

# Notes on Supplementary Modules

1\. Units VIII to XI are supplementary enrichment components.

2\. They are intended to connect traditional mensuration methods with current forest-inventory and monitoring practices.

3\. Their inclusion should not reduce the time required to develop competence in fundamental tree-measurement skills.

4\. Practical exposure should be prioritized over highly detailed theoretical treatment of advanced technologies.

5\. Forest inventory exercises should emphasize fixed-area sample plots.

6\. The relascope and wedge prism are not included in the revised module.

7\. Canopy cover assessment follows the Forest Canopy Cover Assessment Guidelines 2024 (FMID, DoFPS), using the GRS crown densitometer. The spherical densiometer is retained as a comparative instrument and for teaching the reduction of counts.

8\. Laser rangefinders and digital hypsometers may be used to supplement clinometer-based height measurement.

9\. Where equipment is unavailable, lecturers may use demonstrations, sample datasets, photographs, videos, case studies, or previously collected information.

10\. Supplementary modules should only be formally examined or assigned marks when approved through the appropriate academic process.

11\. Supplementary content may be adjusted according to available time, equipment, student readiness, institutional priorities, and field conditions.

12\. The adjunct lecturer should coordinate with the programme leader or module coordinator before making changes to formally assessed content.

13\. Data-analysis exercises should use software available to the institution and accessible to students.

14\. Advanced technologies such as drones and LiDAR should be introduced conceptually unless adequate equipment, software, trained personnel, and practical time are available.

15\. All field activities must follow institutional safety requirements.
